Why it works

Built for players and club pros: play by yourself, with a partner, or run it as a two-target drill.

How it works

Land it. Count it. Reach 21.

Place the mat in the corner of the service box you want to target and serve. Wherever the ball lands is what it scores. Call out the ball number and your running total as you go.

  • 5Red — the corner. The serve you want.
  • 3Green — the middle band.
  • 1Yellow — the outside band.

21+ points in 12 balls

That's the goal for every try. Hit it consistently and it's time to make things harder.

Flip it over

The reverse side of the mat has smaller targets. Once you're scoring 21 with 12 serves consistently on all four target positions, turn the targets over for a bigger challenge.

Ways to play

Solo, with a partner, or as a club drill

1 player

Solo Practice

  1. Place the target in the backhand corner of the serve you want to improve — likely your opponent's backhand side.
  2. Serve a total of 12 balls, adding up the points.
  3. Switch to the other backhand side for another 12.
  4. Then practice serves to the forehand sides.
  5. See if you hit a cumulative 21 points each try.

Only one target? A partner can call out balls and scores and return your serves — or do it yourself.

2 players · club pro format

Two Players, Two Targets

  1. Set up two opposing targets, one for each player.
  2. Alternate serving three balls at a time until 12 balls have been played.
  3. Each player calls out the ball number and the cumulative score.
  4. Record it on the printable scoresheet.
  5. Scoring 21 in 12 consistently on all four positions? Turn the targets over to the smaller side.

Variations

10balls to reach 21

Advanced Challenge

  • Orient the target in any one of four places on both the forehand and backhand sides.
  • Switch to the smaller targets on the reverse side.
  • Make up your own challenges as your accuracy develops.

Dinking Drills

Place the targets in and around the kitchen line and practice placing your dinks into the zones. Two targets are optimal for two players practicing with each other.

Serving strategy

Serve to the weakness

When warming up, probe your opponent's weakness. Normally, it is the backhand of the weakest player on your opponent's side. Your serves — and subsequent play — should target that weakness. Should they return your serve with a weak shot, either you or your partner will be in a better position to win the point.

Serving hint

For the best angle to a backhand, stand as close to the center line as possible without stepping outside or inside the baseline, then serve straight up the center to your opponent's backhand.

From the other side, stand as close as possible to the corner and serve diagonally as deep as possible into the corner of your opponent's court.

If your opponent stands too far over to protect the backhand by running around it, serve straight up the opposite side of the service box.

Server Receiver (R right-handed, L left-handed) Mat sizes illustrative

5 Red rectangular sideline targets

A large (2 ft wide) and small (1.5 ft wide) rectangular 5-point target for practicing difficult side-court shots. Place them on the far right or left sideline from the kitchen line down. Points here are even more challenging and depend on where your opponent is standing.

Fold the red targets into halves, quarters, or just about any size you want to create the ultimate target challenge.

Running around the backhand?

If your opponent stands too far to the left or right to protect the backhand by running around it to hit a forehand, you can always serve it straight up the opposite side of the service box.

      Care & equipment

      Pick your pieces

      Targets in two sizes and a 20-ball pick-up tube — each available separately. Ask your club trainer/professional, or get in touch with RCI.

      PickleServe™ 21 target

      The fan target with red 5, green 3 and yellow 1 zones. Turn it over for the smaller targets on the reverse side when you're ready for more of a challenge.

      Red sideline targets

      A large (2 ft wide) and small (1.5 ft wide) rectangular 5-point target for difficult side-court serves. Fold them into halves, quarters, or just about any size you want.

      20-ball pick-up tube

      Holds 20 balls — enough for a full 12-ball trial and then some. Collect your serves between trials for your own practice.

      Rolling & storage

      When rolling the PickleServe™ 21 mat, always roll in the direction away from the red target on the target's long side.

      If curling is observed on the side closest to the server, lightly bend the target to straighten it or tuck it underneath so that it lies flat.

      Indoor & outdoor play

      For indoor or outdoor play.

      When dirty, simply clean mats with soap and water.
